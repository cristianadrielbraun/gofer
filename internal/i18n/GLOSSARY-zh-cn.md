# Simplified Chinese glossary

These are the words Gofer's Simplified Chinese catalog (`locales/zh-cn.json`) uses for its core terms. Keep new messages consistent with them.

The text addresses the reader as 你, never 您, in the plain, direct register of everyday software in mainland China. Use full-width punctuation in Chinese text (，。：；！？（）) and Chinese quotation marks (“”) around names, but keep the half-width colon in mail header labels ("发件人:"), as mail programs write them. Put a half-width space between Chinese characters and Latin letters, digits or placeholders ("3 封邮件", "Gmail 账户", "{count} 个文件夹"), and none next to full-width punctuation. Lists put 、 between names and 和 before the last one ("邮件、日历 和 联系人"). Write "…" as one character, as English does. Product and protocol names (Gofer, Gmail, Outlook, iCloud, IMAP, SMTP, JMAP, CalDAV, CardDAV, OAuth) stay in English, and so do the names Gofer gives its own features: Gofer Sync, Gofer Local, Web Push and the Express style.

Chinese has no plural forms, so a message's ".one" and ".other" forms read the same. Counted things take their measure word: 封 for mail, 个 for accounts, folders and most other things, 位 for people, 条 for rules and log entries.

## Dates and numbers

Year, month and day in that order, marked with 年, 月 and 日 ("2026年11月2日"), and the 24-hour clock. Months are written as numbers ("11月"). Weekdays are 星期一 in long dates and 周一 in short ones. On the 12-hour clock, 上午 or 下午 comes before the time ("下午3:04"). Numbers use a dot for decimals and a comma for thousands.
